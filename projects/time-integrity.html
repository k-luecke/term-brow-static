Can a shared planetary field provide an exterior clock-integrity observable?

A preregistered six-hour LIGO O3 study across the 3,002 km Hanford–Livingston vault magnetometer baseline, paired with companion WWLLN lightning-transient follow-up tests.

v2.0.0 PREPRINTP1 & P2 SUPPORTEDD2b NOT SUPPORTED

Question

Two-way packet exchange cannot separate asymmetric path delay from clock offset using timestamps alone. The study asks whether the globally shared Schumann resonance magnetic wavefield (6–25 Hz) supplies an independent physical audit observable.

Method

Four subterranean LIGO vault magnetometer channels over 21,600 seconds (GPS 1264298000–1264319600, 128 Hz decimated, twelve 30-minute windows). Two-axis canonical coherence recovers wavefield coupling without compass or gain calibration, evaluated against off-band and phase-randomized surrogate nulls fixed before scoring.

Supported gates

P1 passes strongly: fundamental canonical coherence reaches γ²can(f₁) = 0.3291 (z = 24.54 above the empirical off-band null μ₀ = 0.0399 ± 0.0118). P2 confirms a 49.9× null-corrected vault SNR gain over an unshielded GW170814 pilot. D1 establishes a six-hour Carter Cramér–Rao timing floor of στ = 76.6 µs (45.7 µs raw).

Failure retained

D2b is not supported. Eleven of twelve 30-minute windows are individually significant and median lag (−3.85 ms) lies within the light-time bound, but inter-window scatter (MADN = 4.953 ms) misses the frozen 0.796 ms stationarity threshold by 6.2× due to diurnal thunderstorm-source migration. Companion WWLLN transient-selection tests in Geo also remained negative.

Provenance & credits

Frozen preregistrations, SHA-256 manifests, scored JSON outputs, and reproduction scripts live in-tree. Acknowledges GWOSC (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA) for public O3 magnetometry and Professor Robert H. Holzworth / WWLLN (http://wwlln.net) for global lightning stroke data.

Limits

Reported strictly as an exterior clock-integrity audit bound — not a positioning system, timing service, or GNSS replacement.
